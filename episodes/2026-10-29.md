# Silent Train targets controllers; PHP2Shell roots firewalls

*Volt Typhoon's Silent Train links management-plane intrusions across hundreds of victims, while a completed PHP2Shell chain has rooted firewalls. New exploitation deadlines join the patch queue, and Tailscale's awaited release has landed.*

Good morning, it's 5:22 AM. Thursday, the twenty-ninth of October, twenty twenty-six.

The VeloCloud attacks we covered on Saturday now have an actor behind them: Lumen's Black Lotus Labs ties them to Volt Typhoon's Silent Train. Hundreds of victims have been notified, including telecoms and critical infrastructure operators. Four confirmed zero-day campaigns reached VeloCloud Orchestrator and Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controller, as well as Versa Director and N-able N-central. A suspected Quest KACE zero-day may have affected more than thirty victims too.

Silent Train rotates through compromised devices, chaining overseas relays to a US exit point. Sessions average about four hours; replacing the chain takes under two minutes. That's what worries me about relying on yesterday's blocked addresses. The prize is the management system, with its trusted route into downstream customers.

And Cisco Talos has shown what stays behind. After an authentication bypass, the KeyDrift implant puts PolyFlame inside vdaemon through modified runit scripts, giving command execution. LittleFilch hooks SSL_read to harvest what Talos assesses is IPsec key material. Patch those management platforms and remove their internet-facing administrative access, but don't stop at patching: on Cisco controllers, hunt modified runit scripts and libdssl.so, along with /var/tmp/vdaemon.bin. Keep central logs long enough to investigate the access that preceded the update.

That same management-plane prize is where PHP2Shell has now delivered a complete chain. Dream says a suspected China-linked actor used a custom, non-public build against firewall consoles running PHP 8 point 0 point 30, then Fragnesia to reach root. Twenty-five servers were compromised, including three at a US government-affiliated organisation. The vendor isn't named. That's an awkward gap for anyone trying to find their exposure.

The console's high memory limit helped the PHP memory-corruption stages succeed. A reverse SSH tunnel then reached internal firewall management, where the attacker decrypted an LDAP service-account password and went after Active Directory. Dream says twenty-two other firewalls still carry web shells, and a tunnel is likely still running. It also found AI development tools on the attack infrastructure; the stolen credentials matter rather more to me. Pull exposed firewall management interfaces off the internet and investigate persistence. Fragnesia, CVE-2026-46300, entered KEV yesterday with an October thirty-first federal deadline; VMware ESX's insufficient-logging flaw entered alongside it, but its deadline is November eleventh. Wordfence still has no confirmed WordPress compromises.

The deadline queue has moved beyond PHP too: Node2Shell is now in KEV, due November tenth for federal civilian agencies, with three sensor networks seeing attempts, although ELLIO's captured final stage failed fifty tests. Broadcom's affected SDDC Manager still awaits patches, so use its workarounds; newly fixed releases include Bun 1 point 4 point 4 and brotli4j 1 point 24 point 0. And the Oracle E-Business Suite SQL injection now has exploratory exploits hitting Defused's decoys, without confirmed successful compromise. Apply October's Critical Patch Update, or remove /OA_HTML/AppsTCFServer from the EBS URL firewall allowlist while restricting the web tier.

For shop operators, PolyShell has also entered KEV, with a November tenth federal deadline for the Adobe Commerce and Magento flaw. Patch it, and check CMS blocks for the loader before calling the store clean.

At least one wait in the patch queue is over: Tailscale 1 point 104 point 4 has landed, and SSH forwarding now runs as the logged-in user rather than root. Update SSH servers; headscale 0 point 29 point 5 still doesn't resolve the reported issues, so keep forwarding disabled there. Less cheerful news for backup operators: four critical flaws affect Dell PowerProtect Data Domain and its Management Center, including unauthenticated root command execution and privileged-account creation; credential exposure is another route in, while the Management Center bypass could reach other managed systems. Upgrade the Feature Release line to 8 point 9 point 0 point 0, LTS twenty twenty-four to 7 point 13 point 1 point 90, LTS twenty twenty-five to 8 point 3 point 1 point 50, or LTS twenty twenty-six to 8 point 6 point 1 point 30.

And to keep that real patch work visible, Mozilla's archive replay has added thirty more advisories, now including individual bugs rather than just memory-safety batches. Widen archive suppression down to MFSA 2012-03; these were fixed in twenty twelve, not overnight.

I'd put exposed management consoles ahead of the rest of the morning's queue. And where a patch has already gone on, I'd want evidence that the controller hasn't kept an unwanted passenger. Hope your Thursday leaves you some breathing room.
