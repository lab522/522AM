Monday, the seventh of September, twenty twenty-six. 522 AM is brought to you by Lab522, a technology partner for mid-market businesses, providing cyber security services and enterprise operational software. Email hello at lab522 dot co dot uk if you think we can help your business. If you run Dell Secure Connect Gateway, there's a remote command-execution fix I'd put near the front of today's queue.

Here's the remote route: CVE-2026-61410 is missing authorisation, scoring nine point four, reachable over the network with no login and no click. A crafted request bypasses the restrictions on command execution. For Secure Connect Gateway five, upgrade the application to 5 point 36 point 00 point 00 or later, and the appliance to 5 point 36 point 00 point 16 or later.

That same upgrade also closes CVE-2026-80238, a nine point three unnecessary-privileges flaw with a local route to root. A low-privileged operator with SSH access can use an exposed Docker socket to gain root without a password. Different entrance, same uncomfortable destination; both fixes come with that gateway upgrade.

From a gateway into a host to a gateway into your identity system: CVE-2026-76578 affects FreeIPA and scores nine point eight. Its self-managed OTP token access rule doesn't require authentication and doesn't limit the attributes added alongside a token. Combine that with a separately tracked directory-server access-rule flaw, and an unauthenticated LDAP client can create an attacker-controlled Kerberos principal and get it added to the administrators group.

The catch worth keeping straight is that it's a chain, not a claim that the OTP rule alone does everything. It's network-reachable, with no credentials or click required; Red Hat Enterprise Linux seven and ten are named, but there's no precise affected range or fixed version to give you yet. If you operate FreeIPA, check the advisory against your deployment: an OTP enrolment path becoming an administrator factory is the sort of identity failure I'd lose sleep over.

Now to the network devices, starting with CVE-2026-86296 in D-Link DIR-822A firmware A_101. It's a stack-based buffer overflow in udhcpcd's serverpacket.c, involving strcpy, and it scores ten: network access, no login, no click. An exploit is public, and D-Link haven't identified safe firmware here. Check for that exact model and firmware rather than treating every DIR-822 as interchangeable.

Staying with network kit, CVE-2026-86299 affects Linksys RE7000 firmware 2 point 0 point 15. Its PingTest handler lets the IP address, packet size or test-count parameters become OS command injection through the JSON CGI endpoint. The score's nine point nine, but unlike the D-Link flaw, this needs low-privilege credentials; it's remote and doesn't need a click. The exploit's public, with no fixed version announced here — a ping diagnostic really shouldn't double as a command console.

There's a similar authenticated command-injection pair in Advantech's WISE gateways, both scoring nine point nine, both remote, and neither needing a click. The affected firmware is 1 point 2 point 1, build two zero two five one one one zero, across WISE-6610 variants NB, EB, TB, JB and CB, their EL equivalents, and WISE-6610P variants DEA, DNA and DTA. Both exploits are public.

Of those two, the actionable upgrade is for CVE-2026-79698: the act argument reaches command injection in the Node-RED Library handler. Upgrade to 1 point 2 point 4, build two zero two six zero eight two one. The companion flaw uses the act argument in the Basic Station certificate-deletion handler, but a fix for that one hasn't been identified. Don't assume the Node-RED fix closes both doors.

Which brings us from newly disclosed network flaws to routers already being taken over. Attackers are chaining two recently disclosed MikroTik RouterOS vulnerabilities against devices with internet-exposed SSH; the chain includes an SSH authentication bypass. MikroTik released a patch late last week, although there's no safe version number to quote today. If that exposure matches a RouterOS deployment, patch and investigate for compromise — SANS is advising you to assume compromise, not simply install the update and declare victory.

The same urgency carries into remote management, where N-able has issued its fourth N-central hotfix in five weeks. Every on-premises build below 2026 point 3 point 1 point 14 needs Hotfix Four, including servers updated to Hotfix Three just a day earlier. The issue is unauthenticated remote code execution at maximum severity. Yesterday's successful change ticket isn't the finish line, unfortunately.

There's also a contradiction you shouldn't have to untangle during an emergency change: N-able's incident notice says exploitation has happened, while its release notes call that unconfirmed. I'd move to Hotfix Four rather than let that wording dispute slow the work. The affected range and the required build are clear.

While we're on remote access, ConnectWise has warned of a new ScreenConnect Remote Access flaw, with a patch planned for later this week. Temporary mitigations are available from ConnectWise, so use those vendor measures if you operate the affected product rather than waiting for the patch. The affected versions and the mitigation steps themselves haven't been specified in today's announcement coverage.

Separately, ScreenConnect is being abused in a worm-like campaign: backdoored instances transfer and execute payloads on newly connected clients. The payload delivery includes a four-stage VBScript chain. For ScreenConnect operators, check what newly connected hosts have received and executed; the remote-support path is doing the distribution work. There's no established connection here to the newly announced vulnerability.

From remote support to storefronts, attackers are exploiting the StyleSmuggler zero-day to execute code and install a stealthy backdoor on Adobe Commerce and Magento stores. Nobody's given affected version numbers or a fix yet. If you operate those stores, investigate for backdoor activity as well as watching for remediation; an exploited store doesn't become trustworthy merely because an update eventually lands.

Another web application story has a rather important qualification: a public TantoSec exploit chains a Telerik UI for ASP.NET AJAX AES-CBC padding oracle into unauthenticated remote code execution. It only works against applications in a specific non-default configuration, and Progress patched the chain in July. Check the July fixes and whether your application meets the exploit's configuration requirements. Public unauthenticated RCE deserves attention; pretending every Telerik deployment is equally exposed doesn't help anyone.

The quieter server-side story is worth a look too: North Korean hackers are deploying a Linux espionage toolkit that embeds a backdoor in HAProxy. The targets named are automotive and media organisations in South Korea, with long-term surveillance as the aim. If you defend that sector and geography, put HAProxy itself in the investigation scope, not just the applications behind it. The proxy isn't necessarily an innocent bystander.

Finally, two breaches show how much customer data can sit outside the main product. Mathspace says attackers breached its internal Metabase reporting system and stole data relating to more than a million students, staff and parents. If you operate a learning platform with internal reporting, check what personal data that reporting layer can reach. The internal label isn't a security boundary.

And at Trezor, the August breach at shipping and logistics provider ShipMonk now affects eighty-one thousand customers, including an additional sixty-seven thousand US customers. That's a logistics-provider breach, not evidence that the hardware wallets themselves were compromised. For a wallet business, customer records held for fulfilment are still sensitive records — the risk doesn't stop at the device.

I'd get N-central below the Hotfix Four build upgraded and move the affected Dell gateways to their fixed releases, while investigating internet-exposed MikroTik SSH deployments for compromise. Keep an eye on ConnectWise's promised patch later this week, with its temporary measures in place where applicable. 522 AM, brought to you by Lab522. Find us at lab522 dot co dot uk.
