Good morning, it's 5:22 AM. N-able has issued its fourth N-central hotfix in five weeks, and even servers updated a day earlier need another pass. The finish line has moved again.

522 AM is brought to you by Lab522, at lab522 dot co dot uk.

It's Monday, the seventh of September, twenty twenty-six. Today: critical gateway flaws, a route into FreeIPA administrators, urgent N-central and MikroTik updates, and ScreenConnect trouble on two separate fronts.

Today's material provides no EPSS probabilities, KEV status or due dates, or ransomware links for the new CVEs. That's missing information, not evidence that nobody's exploiting them.

Start with Dell Secure Connect Gateway, where there's a definite upgrade target. CVE-2026-61410 is missing authorisation, scoring nine point four: over the network, low complexity, no credentials and no user interaction. A crafted request can bypass code-execution restrictions and execute commands remotely.

The affected products are Secure Connect Gateway 5 point 0 Application before 5 point 36 point 00 point 00, and Appliance before 5 point 36 point 00 point 16. Dell recommends upgrading at the earliest opportunity. Those are separate version targets, so check which package you're actually running.

The same releases also fix CVE-2026-80238, unnecessary privileges, scoring nine point three. Its vector says local access, low complexity, no existing privileges and no user interaction, with impact crossing a security boundary; the description is more specific, naming a low-privileged operator with SSH access. That operator can use an exposed Docker socket to gain root without a password. The socket is doing rather more than opening containers.

FreeIPA has a particularly unpleasant identity chain in CVE-2026-76578, scoring nine point eight. This is missing authentication, reached over the network with low complexity, no credentials and no click. The self-managed OTP token access rule neither requires authentication nor limits which attributes accompany a new token entry.

Combined with a separately tracked directory-server access-rule evaluation flaw, that lets an unauthenticated LDAP client create an attacker-controlled Kerberos principal and add it to the administrators group. An enrolment path becomes an admin-creation path. The affected-product data names Red Hat Enterprise Linux 7 and 10, but supplies neither precise package ranges nor a fix, and doesn't identify the companion flaw. If you operate FreeIPA, check the package-level advisory before deciding your deployment is covered.

D-Link DIR-822A firmware A_101 has CVE-2026-86296, a stack-based buffer overflow in the udhcpcd component's strcpy handling in serverpacket dot c. It scores ten: network access, low complexity, no login, no user interaction, and impact beyond the vulnerable component's security boundary. An exploit is public, but the material gives no fixed firmware or mitigation. Check that exact model and firmware; the maximum score doesn't tell us which deployments expose the vulnerable path.

Two Advantech command-injection flaws affect WISE-6610 firmware 1 point 2 point 1, build 20251110. The named variants are NB, EB, TB, JB and CB, their EL equivalents, and WISE-6610P variants DEA, DNA and DTA. Both have public exploits, but only one has a stated fix in this material.

CVE-2026-79697 scores nine point nine and reaches the Basic Station certificate-deletion handler through its act argument. It's over the network, low complexity, requires low privileges and no user interaction, with impact crossing a security boundary. No fix is specified for this one.

CVE-2026-79698 also scores nine point nine, this time through the act argument in the Node-RED library handler. Its route is over the network, low complexity, with low privileges, no user interaction and impact across a security boundary. Upgrading to 1 point 2 point 4, build 20260821, resolves this flaw; don't assume that statement also covers the certificate-deletion issue.

Linksys RE7000 firmware 2 point 0 point 15 has CVE-2026-86299, OS command injection in its PingTest handler. The IP address, packet-size and repeat-count arguments are the inputs involved: a diagnostic tool with rather too much freedom. It scores nine point nine, over the network with low complexity, low privileges, no user interaction and impact crossing a security boundary. The exploit is public; no fix or mitigation is supplied.

For the Bahçelievler Municipality BiHayat app, CVE-2026-6223 is insufficient restriction of authentication attempts, allowing authentication bypass. It scores nine point four: over the network, low complexity, no credentials and no user interaction. The affected range starts at 2 point 1 point 7 and ends at the supplied marker 07092026, whose version format isn't explained. No fix is given, and the disclosure says the vendor didn't respond.

Now the operational news, starting with N-central. Every on-premises build below 2026 point 3 point 1 point 14 needs Hotfix Four, including servers moved to Hotfix Three a day earlier. This addresses an unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaw described as maximum severity. If you run an affected build, the previous hotfix isn't the destination.

There's a reporting wrinkle worth keeping intact: N-able's incident notice says exploitation has occurred, while its release notes say that remains unconfirmed. BleepingComputer also reports ongoing attacks. That disagreement is not a reason to postpone the supplied fix; it is a reason not to claim the exploitation picture is settled.

MikroTik routers with internet-exposed SSH are being hijacked through a chain of two recently disclosed RouterOS flaws. SANS describes an already-exploited SSH authentication bypass and says a patch arrived late last week. The supplied reports don't give the affected or fixed versions, so check MikroTik's release information for the exact target. For exposed devices, SANS's advice is to assume compromise: this isn't just a version-checking exercise.

Adobe Commerce and Magento stores are being backdoored through the StyleSmuggler zero-day, which allows code execution. The material gives no affected range, patch, entry conditions or indicators. If you operate either platform, this is an active-exploitation report to follow, but there's not enough here to prescribe a particular upgrade or a reliable backdoor check.

ScreenConnect has two distinct stories, and they shouldn't be welded together. ConnectWise has disclosed a new Remote Access vulnerability, with a patch planned later this week and temporary mitigations already shared. Those measures and affected versions aren't reproduced here, so consult that vendor notice rather than guessing at a workaround.

Separately, researchers report worm-like activity using backdoored ScreenConnect instances to transfer and execute payloads on newly connected clients, including a four-stage VBScript chain. Joining a remote-support service becomes the delivery event. If you're investigating ScreenConnect activity, check payload transfers and execution around new client connections. The supplied material doesn't establish that this campaign uses the newly disclosed flaw.

There's also a public TantoSec exploit chaining a Telerik UI for ASP.NET AJAX AES-CBC padding oracle into unauthenticated remote code execution. The important brake on that headline: it requires a specific, non-default application configuration, and Progress patched the chain in July. Check the July fix and configuration requirements if you run Telerik; the exact versions and settings aren't supplied here. Public exploit does not mean every installation is reachable.

Two breaches show how much customer data can sit one service away from the main product. Mathspace says attackers stole data concerning more than one million students, staff and parents after breaching its internal Metabase reporting system. The supplied account doesn't identify the stolen fields, entry method or affected software versions, so it doesn't establish a Metabase vulnerability.

Trezor's August breach at shipping and logistics provider ShipMonk now affects eighty-one thousand customers, including an additional sixty-seven thousand US customers. That's a logistics-provider breach; this material doesn't establish compromise of wallets or keys. For either incident, check the provider's notification for the actual data exposure rather than filling the gaps with the worst imaginable outcome.

Finally, a Linux espionage toolkit attributed to North Korean hackers embeds a backdoor in HAProxy for long-term surveillance of automotive and media organisations in South Korea. The notable mechanism is the backdoor living inside the proxy, rather than being described as a separate service. No initial-access route or detection indicators are supplied; if you're investigating that targeting, HAProxy belongs in the examination.

Priority order: patch affected N-central servers to Hotfix Four, establish the correct MikroTik patched release for exposed routers, and move Dell gateways to the fixed Application or Appliance version. Apply the stated Advantech Node-RED fix where relevant, then check FreeIPA advisories, ScreenConnect's temporary measures and Telerik's July fix against your deployment. Watch for Adobe's StyleSmuggler response and fixes for the remaining public-exploit device flaws.

Thanks for listening. I hope your next maintenance window has a finish line that stays put.
