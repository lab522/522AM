# Locked NAS boxes and a poisoned security update

*Ransomware crews are exploiting old Synology DSM installations, while a hijacked cPGuard update has compromised Linux hosting servers. British refrigeration operators join the attack tally, and there are new fixes and deadlines for previously covered vulnerabilities.*

Good morning, it's 5:22 AM. Wednesday, the fourth of November, twenty twenty-six.

The box holding your backups could be the thing that needs rescuing. Synology says attackers are exploiting a DSM vulnerability it patched in December twenty twenty-four, and customers are reporting C3RB3R ransomware leaving files with a dot L-zero-C-K-three-D extension. DSM is the operating system on Synology's NAS appliances, often doing quiet duty as shared storage or a backup destination. I'd check those builds before coffee. A backup you can't read isn't much of an escape route.

Synology hasn't identified which flaw is being used. Its linked advisory includes a network-reachable code-execution bug in the system plugin daemon, caused by improper output encoding, but that doesn't establish it's the route into these attacks. Once running, the ransomware skips twenty-seven directories and certain system-file types, leaving read-me3 dot txt ransom notes in encrypted directories.

The minimum fixed builds are quite specific. For DSM 7 point 2 point 2, it's 7 point 2 point 2 dash 72806 dash 1; for 7 point 2 point 1, it's 7 point 2 point 1 dash 69057 dash 6. On 7 point 2, use 7 point 2 dash 64570 dash 4 or later. The older branches need 7 point 1 point 1 dash 42962 dash 7, or 6 point 2 point 4 dash 25556 dash 8. Synology also recommends automatic updates.

Except, painfully, the next attack arrived through automatic updates. Compromised credentials let an attacker into OpsShield's cPGuard distribution infrastructure on Monday, pushing a malicious script in versions 5 point 88 point 12 through 5 point 88 point 19. That's a security add-on running as root on Linux hosting servers. The script installed a shell and collected password hashes alongside root's database configuration and shell history, uploading the haul to OpsShield's own licensing server. The vendor says two thousand nine hundred and forty-six servers were affected; the attacker retrieved thirty-two server collections, and copied its billing database too.

Update cPGuard to 5 point 88 point 25 or later, then check its install log for affected versions. What bugs me here is that the fix removes the backdoor and collection directory: their absence afterwards proves nothing. From a trusted environment, reset affected account passwords and rotate exposed secrets, including root's SSH private keys. Removing the collector doesn't recall what it collected.

And from stolen server access to physical disruption: yesterday's refrigeration attacks now include three British abattoirs and wholesaler Langridge Organic. The abattoirs were reached through Copeland XWEB controllers, and there's now a named weakness: authentication bypass in firmware 1 point 12 point 1 and earlier, with an update available since August. That wait has become expensive. Get exposed controllers off public addresses and update XWEB past that release before restoring protected remote access. Check defrost schedules when reconfiguring affected units. The NCSC spent the night contacting exposed UK organisations and says national impact is minimal so far; Belgium's federal prosecutor has opened an investigation. Investigators haven't established a link with October's French attacks.

Back on servers, there's movement on the Linux TCP flaw: Red Hat has shipped fixed kernels for RHEL 8, 9 and 10, raising its rating to nine point eight; install them and reboot. Ubuntu's fixes for 22 point 04, 24 point 04 and 26 point 04 remain in the opt-in proposed channel, without a regular-release date. And yesterday's Node-RED story now has a specific fix: for CVE-2026-58115 on Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced industrial gateways, update to 4 point 3 point 4 point 1 or later. Attackers are also planting webshells that survive reboot, so inspect deployed flows for unexpected exec nodes or HTTP endpoints.

For website operators, Saturday's WordPress Core upgrade-window race is now in CISA's exploited catalogue as CVE-2026-119977, with US federal agencies due to fix it by Friday, the sixth of November. The listed range is 4 point 7 through 7 point 1 point 3; move past that and check for compromise during earlier upgrades. There's no patch for the separate CryptoPayment Gateway plugin flaw, which Defused Cyber says attackers are trying to exploit to delete files and extract stored wallet credentials without authentication. Remove affected versions 1 point 2 point 1 through 1 point 2 point 2, then rotate wallet keys and inspect payment settings.

One thing not to mistake for a fresh emergency: Mozilla's advisory replay has reached twenty eleven, including both DigiNotar certificate notices and advisories for Thunderbird and SeaMonkey as well as Firefox. Widen archive-date suppression through MFSA 2011-03 to 2011-59, plus MFSA 2012-01 and 2012-02; their historical exploitation language isn't evidence of attacks today.

I'd give the storage checks first attention, while treating any affected cPGuard installation as a credentials incident, not just an update job. Keep an eye on Canonical's regular kernel channel rather than assuming the testing builds have reached everyone. Hope Wednesday gives you a quieter patch window.
