[serious] A print server became domain administrator in seven minutes at an American high school. GreyNoise and Blackpoint Cyber say a suspected Russian-speaking operator used hundreds of A-I agents to develop and launch attacks against PaperCut N-G and M-F, the servers that manage printing across a site. From the thirty-first of August, agents compared patched and vulnerable builds, then worked through targets found with Netlas. At least four hundred and forty instances were compromised at three hundred and ninety-five organisations; roughly half were in education. Twelve ended with domain administrator access. [short pause] Once the campaign was running, eleven organisations fell in twenty-six seconds.

[curious] The clever bit wasn't a new vulnerability. It was automating the testing and retries across real victims. An authentication bypass chained with remote code execution opened the door; stolen Windows secrets and pass-the-hash helped reach domain controllers. Elsewhere, agents used noPac against still-vulnerable domains, or simply added an administrator where PaperCut itself ran on a domain controller, finishing with D-C-Sync to copy the domain's credential database. [confident] If you expose PaperCut, I'd take it off the public internet and apply the emergency updates for CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 today. Then hunt credential dumping on those hosts and unexpected Domain Admins membership. Check for D-C-Sync or noPac activity too.

[serious] And getting the fix installed needs a little more scepticism at Check Point. Two V-P-N certificate flaws, both nine point eight, can let an unauthenticated attacker run code: CVE-2026-85102 mishandles certificate trust, while CVE-2026-85103 overflows the heap decoding A-S-N point one certificate structures, reaching Quantum management servers as well as gateways. Check Point says it found both internally, with no indication of attacks, but hasn't explained the required conditions. [curious] What bugs me is the rollout: customers report automatic patches missing, and one older branch has neither fix available. Even a disabled V-P-N blade may leave you exposed if certificates remain. [calm] Verify Live Patch actually landed, or install your branch's latest Jumbo Hotfix. The published affected Take numbers aren't fixed releases. Use advisories S-K one zero zero zero one one seven and one zero zero zero one one eight; where no patch is available, follow their mitigation for disabling implied V-P-N rules.

[short pause] [serious] A patch that does arrive can still spoil the morning. Administrators say September's cumulative updates break Remote Desktop Services on Windows Server 2019 and 2022, with 2025 affected too. Servers can work for hours before sessions fail and new connections hang; some need a hard reset. One administrator suspects a deadlock between Remote Desktop and Local Session Manager during logout, but Microsoft hasn't confirmed the cause. It's investigating, with no fix yet. [confident] I'd test a logout, not just a successful login, before calling that rollout healthy. Rolling back reportedly restores access, but removes September's security fixes, so keep that as a last resort on affected terminal servers.

[long pause] [calm] Back to fixes you can deploy: the MikroTik update now has a federal deadline of Sunday, the thirteenth, after CISA added CVE-2026-67277 and CVE-2026-86060 to its exploited catalogue. Move RouterOS to Long-term 6 point 49 point 21 or 7 point 23 point 4, or Stable 7 point 24 point 2; keep S-S-H off the public side while patching. [curious] On phones, the hiding place is more surprising: Group-I-B says Gigabud in Indonesia now uses a helper called Vwork to create an Android work profile containing a tampered banking app, hiding fraud from the bank app's malware checks. Check unexplained Work tabs under Settings, Passwords and accounts; remove the rogue profile and its creator, which can hide its launcher icon.

[serious] Separately, Zimperium's Mantax Otax arrives through malicious Android packages outside Google Play, stealing credentials and messages while harassing victims with overlays and spoken taunts. Its encryption works only on Android nine and older, though the spyware isn't limited that way: on managed phones, investigate dot enc files or unjustified Accessibility access, and isolate and rebuild affected handsets. [short pause] And back on Windows, another day's coverage of ShieldCrash brings no Microsoft fix; Dark Reading places the release in the researcher's continuing campaign against the company. Keep tamper protection on while watching for a fix.

[confident] I'd give exposed PaperCut servers the first maintenance slot today. For Check Point, I'd want the installed patch status in front of me before closing the ticket.
