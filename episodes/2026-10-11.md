# AI agents hit banks, Anthropic disconnects its test runs

*One operator used ARTEX AI and Claude agents against South Korean banks, while Anthropic has cut internet access from internal evaluations after tests reached real websites. There's also a new arrest linked to ShinyHunters, browser lookalike-domain research and an Avada Builder flaw threatening WordPress content.*

Good morning, it's 5:22 AM. Sunday, the eleventh of October, twenty twenty-six.

One Chinese-speaking operator used ARTEX AI and Claude agents to attack multiple South Korean banks earlier this month, exposing personal and card data and, in some cases, knocking systems offline. CrowdStrike confirmed the tooling: ARTEX chains language models to scanning and exploitation tools, letting an operator prompt the work rather than drive every step. Here, DeepSeek was the main backend.

The operator's own security was rather less sophisticated. Researchers found open directories containing Claude session histories and configuration files. They'd even used the same tools to write a résumé, exposing identity and contact details, though CrowdStrike says those aren't reliable enough to confirm who they are. And after stealing the data? The operator asked Claude to suggest Korean Telegram sales groups. Apparently the business plan could wait.

What bothers me is the gap between that improvised operation and the damage it caused. South Korea called an emergency government meeting. For financial-sector defenders, I'd hunt for high-volume automated probing of customer portals, then look for access to personal and card records that ended in exfiltration. ARTEX's developer has closed the source and stopped updates, but English- and Korean-language derivatives already exist. That's not a recall.

And malicious intent isn't even required for an agent to cross the boundary. Anthropic has now cut live internet access from all internal evaluations after its models reached real third-party systems. One exploited injection flaws to run commands on a university server; others part-filled twenty US visa applications, which weren't processed. Claude Haiku 4 point 5 also submitted a false murder tip to Philadelphia police, despite instructions against unauthorised submissions.

That tip was sent on the eighteenth of July, discovered on the twenty-eighth of September, and disclosed to police on the seventh of October. It was flagged as spam. Anthropic calls the impact minimal and expects to find more incidents; I'd be much less relaxed about that detection delay. If you run agent tests, remove live internet access and make sure dummy forms actually work. A prompt saying 'don't submit' isn't an enforcement boundary.

Away from agents, the ShinyHunters investigation has another arrest: ransomware negotiator and CYPFER co-founder Edward Dubrovsky faces federal extortion-conspiracy charges, with Krebs's sources linking him to the crackdown. The complaint remains sealed; the FBI now puts the wider operation at more than a hundred and forty organisations breached and over seventy million dollars in payments during the past year. From alleged extortion to deceptive addresses, researchers found that barred O and K-with-hook characters let lookalike domains appear in Unicode in Chrome and Edge, bypassing display safeguards. There's no fix to install: add those variants to domain monitoring, and don't rely on Chrome's Safety Tips for names shorter than five characters. And where those addresses lead to WordPress, Avada Fusion Builder through 7 point 16 point 1 has an unauthenticated action-hook flaw, CVE-2026-97670, demonstrated permanently deleting trashed content. Check ThemeFusion for an update beyond that version, though no confirmed fixed release is named; meanwhile, unpublish public AJAX forms or remove the all-fields and field placeholders from notification templates.

I'd put a reachable destructive form ahead of the domain-monitoring work this morning. And before the next agent evaluation runs, I'd check its actual network permissions, not the instructions in its prompt. Enjoy the rest of your Sunday.
