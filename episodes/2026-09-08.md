Tuesday, the eighth of September, twenty twenty-six. 522 AM is brought to you by Lab522, a technology partner for mid-market businesses, providing cyber security services and enterprise operational software. Email hello at lab522 dot co dot uk if you think we can help your business. Adobe has released patches for the StyleSmuggler zero-day. That's the important change since yesterday: store operators now have a vendor fix to apply, while attackers are deploying a Rust backdoor and PHP web shells.

The flaw is CVE-2026-75650, and it scores ten. Improper handling of special elements in the template engine lets an attacker execute code as the application's current user, over the network, without a login or a click. That's execution inside the software running your shop, not just somebody changing the window display.

The affected Adobe Commerce branches run from 2 point 4 point 4 through 2 point 4 point 9, including their August twenty twenty-six updates. Magento Open Source branches 2 point 4 point 6 through 2 point 4 point 9 are also listed through those August updates, alongside several Commerce B2B branches. Get Adobe's Monday security patches matched to your edition and branch; an August update doesn't clear you. And because attackers are leaving both native backdoors and web shells, check beyond the PHP files when investigating a store. Closing the entrance doesn't remove whoever came through it.

From storefronts to the systems behind the business: SAP has two new critical flaws worth getting in front of your SAP team today. The more concrete trust failure is in NetWeaver Message Server, which coordinates communication between application servers. It doesn't sufficiently verify that a component registering as an internal application server really belongs there.

An attacker with network access can register an unauthorised component without credentials or user interaction, potentially gaining the ability to perform unauthorised actions inside the application environment. CVE-2026-58240 scores nine point eight and affects versions 9 point 18, 9 point 19 and 9 point 20. The registration desk is accepting claims of membership without checking them. Review which hosts can reach that service and check SAP's remediation guidance for those versions.

Alongside that, SAP's Extended Passport Protocol processing library has a memory-safety flaw triggered by a malformed EPP header in a crafted network request. The library handles context passed between SAP components, so this is embedded plumbing rather than another application for your inventory. CVE-2026-44756 scores ten and needs neither authentication nor user interaction. Affected versions are 7 point 22 EXT, 7 point 53, 8 point 04, and 9 point 18 through 9 point 20.

Here's the distinction: abnormal termination and undefined behaviour are described, with potentially severe confidentiality and integrity consequences, but confirmed code execution isn't. Don't turn the score into a capability nobody's established. Fixed builds haven't been specified for either SAP flaw here; use SAP's current guidance to establish the update destination, and check service reachability while you do.

There's a more straightforward version boundary on the industrial side. Siemens Reyrolle 7SR5 protection relays, the devices monitoring electrical systems and responding to faults, are affected before version 2 point 70. Their web interface exposes information that lets an attacker calculate current and past session IDs. The result is an authentication bypass and unauthorised device access over the network, with no login or click required.

That's CVE-2026-62645, scoring nine point eight. If you operate these relays, arrange the move to 2 point 70 or later under your operational change process, and restrict web-interface access to the management hosts that need it. A session identifier isn't much of a secret if the device hands out the working.

While we're on appliances, there's an additional D-Link DIR-822A flaw, distinct from yesterday's warning. Firmware A underscore 101 has an out-of-bounds write in its L2TP control-message parser, with a public exploit. It requires a low-privilege login over the network. Check D-Link's guidance for CVE-2026-86510 and restrict access to the affected service; no safe firmware has been identified.

Moving from appliance access to persistence on workstations, researchers have disclosed PEEP, a Chromium-based post-exploitation toolkit that disguises itself as a bookmarks extension. It turns Chrome and Edge into backdoors capable of executing commands on the host. The bookmarks are rather less interesting than what sits behind them.

Post-exploitation is the key word: this isn't evidence that visiting a page gives an attacker a shell in a clean browser. For responders investigating an already compromised endpoint, though, browser extensions belong in the persistence review. Don't dismiss one just because its stated job sounds harmless; correlate suspect extensions with unexpected host command execution.

And attackers are also using the browser's front door. BengalSEO is a search-poisoning campaign targeting Bing results to deliver MayaBot malware and tech-support scams. The DFIR Report discovered it in March. For investigations involving those payloads or scams, trace the search result and subsequent browsing path, rather than assuming the lure arrived by email. A prominent search result can lend borrowed credibility just as neatly as a convincing sender name.

One more before we finish, and the scale needs careful wording. An exposed, Vietnam-linked Advance Passenger Information System database held two hundred and twenty million passenger and crew records. Records, not necessarily that many different people. They span twenty seventeen to twenty twenty-six and include names, passport numbers, dates of birth, nationalities and flight details.

APIS systems carry identity and journey information for passenger processing and border checks. Put those fields together and you've got enough context for a very convincing travel-related approach. If you operate passenger-data integrations, review exposure and access controls on the databases carrying that feed, not just the public booking application. The exposure is established; who accessed the database hasn't been established here.

I'd give the Adobe patches the first change slot today, with compromise checks alongside the update rather than afterwards as an optional extra.
