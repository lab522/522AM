[cheerful] Good morning, it's 5:22 AM. Tuesday, the eighth of September, twenty twenty-six. five twenty-two AM is brought to you by Lab five twenty-two, a technology partner for mid-market businesses, providing cyber security services and enterprise operational software. Email us at hello at lab five twenty-two dot co dot uk if you think we can help your business. [serious] Adobe's fix for StyleSmuggler has arrived. Monday's security patches give Commerce and Magento operators a way to close the hole we were waiting on yesterday, with attackers deploying a Rust backdoor and P-H-P web shells. That's an emergency change, not something to tuck into the next shop-front refresh.

[confident] The flaw to track is CVE-2026-75650, scoring ten: reachable over the network, no login, no click. Special elements aren't properly neutralised in the template engine, allowing code execution as the application's current user. Commerce releases on the 2 point 4 point 4 through 2 point 4 point 9 branches, through their August twenty twenty-six updates, are affected; Magento Open Source is listed on branches 2 point 4 point 6 through 2 point 4 point 9. Commerce B-to-B releases are also affected. [calm] Match your installed release and extensions against Adobe's advisory and apply Monday's security patches; the exact fixed build names aren't specified here. And because backdoors are already being planted, check for those alongside the upgrade. Closing the entry point doesn't evict somebody who's moved in.

[short pause] There's also a useful detection follow-up for N-central administrators: check for newly created user accounts you don't recognise. [serious] That's the new action alongside yesterday's hotfix advice. An unexplained account on your remote-management server deserves investigation, not a tidy-up and a closed ticket. Establish who created it and what it's accessed before deciding you've contained the incident.

[curious] From management accounts to a much stranger execution trail: Cisco Talos began investigating ClearFake after a D-L-L named “verification dot google” ran from WebDAV at a Ukrainian government organisation. The infection chain delivers Amatera stealer, ZigCryptoStealer and NetSupport Manager, a remote-control tool. [serious] For detection teams, that gives you something concrete to look for: D-L-L execution from WebDAV, followed by credential-stealing or remote-control activity. A filename that borrows Google's name isn't a signature of trust.

[short pause] And in a separate browser-based cryptocurrency theft campaign, the Google connection is real — just not reassuring. Talos says attackers retrieve obfuscated JavaScript from a publicly published Google Sheets document through the Google Visualisation A-P-I, then inject it into the victim's browser session. [curious] Command and control hiding behind a spreadsheet. That's clever, and deeply inconvenient for anyone treating a familiar cloud domain as the end of an investigation. If you're investigating this ClickFix activity, follow what the script retrieves and does inside the browser; don't clear it simply because the request went to Google.

[long pause] Now to the new flaws, and S-A-P has two that I'd put in front of the application owners today. The first sits in Extended Passport Protocol processing, which carries tracing context between S-A-P components. A malformed E-P-P header in a crafted network request can trigger memory-unsafe behaviour and abnormal programme termination. [serious] CVE-2026-44756 scores ten and needs neither credentials nor a user click. The stated impact extends across confidentiality, integrity and availability, but the description doesn't establish a reliable code-execution chain. A ten is a reason to act, not permission to invent the exploit.

[calm] The affected E-P-P processing versions are 7 point 22 EXT, 7 point 53, 8 point 04, and 9 point 18 through 9 point 20. No fixed versions are named, so check S-A-P's security guidance against the deployed components and review which services processing these requests are reachable from untrusted networks. [confident] Don't let the word “tracing” make this sound harmless: the request still reaches native code inside the application.

[short pause] The second S-A-P flaw is about who gets accepted as part of the application environment. NetWeaver Message Server coordinates application servers, but its component-registration checks don't sufficiently establish that a joining server is genuine. [serious] With CVE-2026-58240, an unauthenticated attacker who can reach the service can register an unauthorised component and potentially perform unauthorised actions within that environment. It scores nine point eight, with no user interaction required. That's a stranger being handed an internal badge at the door.

[calm] Message Server versions 9 point 18, 9 point 19 and 9 point 20 are affected; a fixed release hasn't been identified. Check S-A-P's advisory and limit service reachability to the application servers that need it while you establish remediation. [confident] I'd prioritise exposure checks here: being called an internal service doesn't make a reachable port internal.

[short pause] Over in industrial equipment, Siemens has a much clearer upgrade destination. Reyrolle 7SR5 protection relays sit in electrical power systems, and their web interface exposes information that can be used to calculate current and past session identifiers. [serious] That lets an attacker bypass authentication and gain unauthorised device access over the network, without credentials or a click. CVE-2026-62645 scores nine point eight. For every version before V2 point 70, move to V2 point 70 or later through your site's operational change process. Restrict web-interface access while you arrange it. Predictable sessions are bad enough on a website; on a protection relay, the stakes are rather different.

[short pause] One additional router flaw has appeared since yesterday's D-Link coverage. The DIR-822A on firmware A_101 also has a public exploit for an out-of-bounds write in its L-two-T-P control-message parser. This one requires a low-privilege login over the network, and no click. [calm] D-Link haven't identified safe firmware in today's disclosure. If you run that build, include L-two-T-P reachability in your exposure review rather than looking only at the earlier attack surface.

[long pause] Back to browsers, but with an important distinction: PEEP is a post-compromise toolkit, not a newly disclosed Chrome or Edge entry-point flaw. It masquerades as a bookmarks extension and turns Chromium-based browsers into backdoors for host command execution. [serious] If you're responding to an already compromised endpoint, include browser extensions in the investigation and examine unexpected commands launched from the browser. A bookmarks label doesn't confine an extension to organising tabs.

[short pause] Finally, an exposed Vietnam-linked Advance Passenger Information System database held two hundred and twenty million passenger and crew records. These systems hold information about people travelling on flights; this exposure includes names, passport numbers, dates of birth, nationalities and flight details spanning twenty seventeen to twenty twenty-six. [serious] That's records, not a confirmed count of unique people. And it's an exposure, not proof that every record was stolen. For teams running passenger-information systems, check database reachability and access history: this is identity data tied to years of movements, not merely a leaked mailing list.

[confident] I'd give Adobe's new patches the first change slot, with a compromise investigation running alongside them. Then I'd establish who can reach those S-A-P services and get affected Siemens relays into an operationally safe upgrade window. [cheerful] five twenty-two AM, brought to you by Lab five twenty-two.
