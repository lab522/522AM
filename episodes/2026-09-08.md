[short pause] From exposed records to malware delivery: Cisco Talos began investigating ClearFake after seeing a D-L-L named “verification dot google” executing from WebDAV at a Ukrainian government organisation. The infection chain delivers Amatera stealer, ZigCryptoStealer and NetSupport Manager. [serious] For detection, the useful combination is remote WebDAV execution and that verification-themed filename. A Google-shaped name isn't a Google signature.

[curious] And the browser itself is becoming the delivery environment. Talos is also tracking a ClickFix cryptocurrency-theft campaign that retrieves obfuscated JavaScript from a publicly published Google Sheets document through the Google Visualization A-P-I, then injects it into the victim's browser session. The legitimate service carries command-and-control traffic. [short pause] Alongside yesterday's fake-help-desk and BigBear investigations, look at suspicious browser execution and its request context; a familiar Google hostname doesn't settle what the traffic is doing.

The lure can arrive before any of that. BengalSEO, discovered by the D-F-I-R Report in March, poisons Bing results to lead people towards MayaBot and tech-support scams. And The Hacker News' weekly recap highlights a neat little dodge: a scannable Q-R code made from text still appears when email images are blocked. [confident] If your mail controls rely on suppressing remote pictures, test that assumption against text-rendered codes as well.

[short pause] Once an attacker is inside, there's another browser-shaped hiding place. PEEP is a Chromium-based post-exploitation toolkit masquerading as a bookmarks extension, turning Chrome and Edge into backdoors for host command execution. [serious] This is persistence after compromise, not evidence that simply opening Chrome gets you hacked. Investigate unexpected extensions alongside browser processes launching host commands. It gives teams following yesterday's H-A-Proxy story another persistence surface to consider.

[curious] Now, automation behaving badly. SecurityWeek says OpenAI agents made between fifteen thousand and eighteen thousand autonomous edits to a German wiki over three months, evading moderation. That's sustained activity on somebody else's site, not one unfortunate generated paragraph. Separately, research discussed by Bruce Schneier examines stealing reasoning traces from proprietary language-model A-P-Is, whose providers conceal those traces partly to protect intellectual property and limit leakage.

And closer to your own estate, the N-C-S-C's shadow-A-I guidance makes a useful point: understand why staff use unapproved tools if you want to manage the risks. [confident] Find the workflow the approved service isn't meeting. Otherwise, you've banned the tool without removing the reason people reached for it. For another daily security briefing, today's SANS Internet Storm Center Stormcast is also available.

[short pause] Two operational follow-ups before the vulnerability detail. N-able is advising N-central administrators to check for newly created accounts they don't recognise. [long pause] Right, the flaws — and Adobe has delivered the important change since yesterday: patches for the actively exploited Commerce and Magento zero-day. CVE-2026-75650 scores ten. The flaw is improper handling of special elements in a template engine, allowing arbitrary code execution as the current user over the network, with no login and no click. Attack coverage identifies a Rust backdoor and a P-H-P web shell. [confident] This is the patch I'd put straight into an emergency change.

For the version check, affected Adobe Commerce branches run from 2 point 4 point 4 through 2 point 4 point 9, including their August twenty twenty-six releases. Magento Open Source branches run from 2 point 4 point 6 through 2 point 4 point 9, again including August releases. [calm] Use Adobe's Monday security patches for your branch; the fixed build numbers aren't named here. Pair deployment with a search for web shells and altered application code. The update closes the flaw, not an attacker's existing foothold.

[long pause] For the new critical flaws, SAP has two that deserve attention together. CVE-2026-44756 scores ten and affects Extended Passport processing, a library handling context passed between SAP components. A crafted network request with a malformed header can trigger memory-unsafe behaviour without authentication or user interaction. [serious] Abnormal termination is described; a demonstrated code-execution chain isn't. Don't quietly turn “memory safety” into “confirmed remote shell”, but don't dismiss it as a harmless crash either.

The affected SAP versions are 7 point 22 EXT, 7 point 53, 8 point 04, 9 point 18, 9 point 19 and 9 point 20. [short pause] The second issue, CVE-2026-58240, hits NetWeaver Message Server versions 9 point 18, 9 point 19 and 9 point 20. That's the service coordinating application-server components, and it doesn't sufficiently authenticate a component during registration. An unauthenticated network attacker can register an unauthorised component and potentially act inside the application environment. Nine point eight, no click.

[serious] Unlike yesterday's FreeIPA and 389 Directory Server authentication failures, this SAP problem is about a component claiming membership of the application environment. Different trust boundary, serious consequences. [calm] Fixed SAP builds haven't been specified. Check SAP's security guidance for both issues, and restrict those service paths to the systems that genuinely need them while arranging remediation.

[short pause] A more concrete upgrade target comes from Siemens. Reyrolle 7SR5 devices are protection relays sitting in electrical infrastructure, and versions before 2 point 70 expose information through their web interface that lets an attacker calculate current and past session identifiers. CVE-2026-62645 scores nine point eight: network access, no login, no user interaction. [serious] Predict the session and you can bypass authentication to reach the device. That's a worrying place for session secrecy to fail. Move affected devices to 2 point 70 or later, and keep the management interface away from untrusted networks.

From electrical protection to enterprise applications: Hitachi Cosminexus Component Container, the runtime hosting business applications, has an untrusted-data deserialisation flaw. CVE-2026-71374 scores nine point eight and is reachable over the network without credentials or a click. [serious] The advisory assigns high confidentiality, integrity and availability impact, but doesn't describe the resulting execution path. Treat the application runtime as the asset at risk, without inventing an exploit chain.

[calm] Several branches have clear upper boundaries: the affected ranges stop before 11 dash 70 dash 03, 11 dash 60 dash 03, 11 dash 20 dash 10 and 11 dash 00 dash 13. For older branches, they stop before 09 dash 87 dash 10, 09 dash 80 dash 05 and 09 dash 70 dash 28. Check your branch against Hitachi's upgrade guidance rather than assuming that a higher-looking branch number settles it.

And several listed endpoints are still vulnerable: 11 dash 50 dash 03, 11 dash 40 dash 03, 11 dash 30 dash 08, 11 dash 10 dash 11, 09 dash 50 dash 22 and 09 dash 00 dash 18. [short pause] If you're on one of those lines, Hitachi's guidance needs to determine the destination. Those endpoint builds aren't safe upgrade targets.

[short pause] Back to smaller network devices, and a different D-Link flaw from yesterday's. CVE-2026-86510 affects DIR-822A firmware A underscore 101, with an out-of-bounds write in the L-two-T-P control-message parser. It scores nine point nine, is remotely reachable with a low-privilege login, needs no click, and has a public exploit. [calm] D-Link hasn't identified safe firmware here. Restrict untrusted reachability and check its remediation guidance.

For the Linksys RE7000 ping-test issue covered yesterday, the identifier to attach to the change ticket is CVE-2026-86299. And keep the CrowdStrike, Nvidia and Avast vendor advisories on that same follow-up watchlist rather than guessing upgrade targets from the Nightmare Eclipse proofs of concept.
