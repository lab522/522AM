*ShinyHunters claims it breached FBI services through a PeopleSoft zero-day, while Microsoft disrupts a phishing service linked to twelve thousand compromised accounts. Exploited flaws in F5, Check Point and Arista make for an urgent patch queue, alongside new WordPress and Adobe fixes.*

[cheerful] Good morning, it's 5:22 AM. Wednesday, the twenty-third of September, twenty twenty-six.

[short pause] [serious] ShinyHunters says it's hacked the people investigating it. The group claims an Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day opened FBI services, exposing employee and job-applicant data, including agents' home addresses. It claims more than two terabytes, and wants the FBI to retract its description of the gang rather than pay. The FBI is investigating; the claimed exploit and the scale aren't confirmed. [calm] I'd keep those distinctions firmly attached to any internal briefing. If the personal information is genuine, the danger goes well beyond embarrassment: it gives someone leverage over agents and their families.

And other services are already showing visible interference. Sorami Consulting says connections to Elsevier's Evolve and Sherpath services, alongside ClinicalPharmacology and G-S-D-D interfaces, have been redirected to LAPSUS$-linked extortion pages. [serious] Separately, attackers accessed BigCommerce customer data using an application key compromised at Ribon Apps. That's the uncomfortable bit about integrations: the platform doesn't have to fall for its customer data to leave.

[curious] The same problem turns up in development access. CrowdSec lost a hundred and seventy private GitHub repositories after an OAuth token was stolen from a former employee's computer through the TanStack npm supply-chain attack. I'd check surviving tokens from departed staff, not just whether their accounts were disabled. And developers integrating Twilio should look for the malicious npm package tw-pkgprobe-7731, posing as a bug-bounty probe while attempting to harvest sensitive data. A security label on a package isn't permission to run it.

[short pause] [serious] The consequences keep arriving long after the intrusion. Sweden has fined systems provider Miljödata a hundred and eighty-three thousand dollars over inadequate security behind last August's breach affecting two point two million people. Canada's privacy regulator is investigating IDScan's security and whether its victim notifications were adequate. In court, a Ryuk ransomware participant received two years in prison and three years of supervised release for attacks on American companies.

[calm] That leaves the question of who tells whom when things go wrong. European Union auditors say information-sharing gaps are hampering major incident response. Dark Reading's argument for closer security and marketing teams fits here: agree crisis communications before there's an extortion page to explain. Not the morning to discover that nobody owns the customer message.

[short pause] [cheerful] There is a disruption worth welcoming: Microsoft says EvilTokens helped compromise twelve thousand Microsoft accounts over a few months, and it's now seized fifty websites and disabled more than a hundred and fifty domains. The device-code phishing service used A-I for target selection and social-engineering messages, with Microsoft describing its use throughout the attack chain. [serious] For Microsoft 365 defenders, a takedown isn't a session revocation. I'd check device-code sign-ins during any related account investigation rather than assume the infrastructure seizure evicted the attacker.

And not every lure needs a new service. Alongside today's SANS Stormcast, its handlers have documented the Macfinger ClickFix campaign injecting scripts into legitimate websites. That makes the trusted page the delivery point for the persuasion. [curious] There's also research showing how an attacker with privileged access can register a rogue external M-F-A provider and steal passwords during legitimate logins. The privilege requirement matters: this is abuse of an authentication configuration, not a claim that anyone can bypass M-F-A from outside.

[serious] Once inside a Windows machine, ClosedQuorum takes the A-I idea further: it queries language models to choose from predefined post-compromise actions, including credential and cryptocurrency-wallet theft. It can use four providers. The important limitation is 'predefined'—the malware is selecting actions, not inventing an unlimited new capability.

[curious] Access to those models has its own murky infrastructure. Risky Business describes ten thousand A-I servers masking Chinese malicious activity; Dark Reading describes more than eighty thousand relay servers masking Chinese users' identities to access frontier models, possibly for cloning. Those are different claims, not counts I'd add together. And new self-jailbreaking research finds reasoning models can reason their way out of safety alignment after otherwise benign reasoning training. That gives the wider coverage of rogue-model incidents, and debates about catastrophic A-I scenarios, something more concrete to examine than predictions alone.

[calm] The proposed controls are arriving too. Outerlimit has raised sixteen million dollars for a decentralised authorisation layer intended to block harmful autonomous actions, while Okta has announced agent lifecycle controls and a kill switch. A proposed American bill would give critical-infrastructure operators free access to frontier models through a CISA test programme. None of those announcements establishes that the safety problem is solved.

For visibility into what actually happens, The Register makes the case for putting security in the network, while a Wazuh article focuses on finding shadow I-T outside existing monitoring. [confident] My preference is evidence of what ran and where it connected over another promise about what an agent ought to do.

[long pause] [serious] And the network equipment itself needs attention before any of that. Four newly listed exploited flaws carry a federal deadline of Friday, September twenty-fifth. For F5 BIG-IP Access Policy Manager, CVE-2026-94127 is a heap overflow giving an unauthenticated attacker code execution—but the configuration is crucial. It affects a virtual server with an access policy and OAuth profile when A-P-M acts as an OAuth authorisation server. Client-only or resource-server-only deployments without those authorisation profiles aren't affected. Appliance mode doesn't protect you.

[calm] F5 has engineering hotfixes: for the 21 point 1 branch, 21 point 1 point 0 point 2 point 0 point 30 point 22; for 17 point 5, 17 point 5 point 1 point 9 point 0 point 160 point 12. On 17 point 1, it's 17 point 1 point 3 point 5 point 0 point 41 point 14. [confident] I'd establish the OAuth role before coffee, then apply the matching hotfix if it's exposed. Active exploitation makes this more than a configuration tidy-up.

[serious] The same deadline covers two separate Check Point problems. CVE-2026-85102 is improper certificate trust validation during V-P-N negotiation in Quantum Security Gateway, allowing remote code execution without authentication. The other, CVE-2026-93616, lets someone reaching Security Management Server's web service traverse directories and upload scripts they can execute without logging in. Check Point says that one was used in a handful of targeted attacks on July twenty-third, and emergency hotfixes are available. [confident] What bugs me is the time gap. For the management-server flaw, I'd investigate historical web-service access as well as install the fix; the box controlling security policy deserves both.

And the fourth is the controller for a wide-area network. Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator's CVE-2026-93952 lets remote attackers reach privileged internal functionality and compromise the orchestrator host. [calm] Affected on-premises ranges start at 5 point 2 point 0 and run through 5 point 2 point 3 point 15, and from 6 point 1 point 0 through 6 point 1 point 3 point 7. The other ranges are 6 point 4 point 0 through 6 point 4 point 2 point 7, and 7 point 0 point 0 through 7 point 0 point 0 point 2. Arista urges immediate patching; hosted deployments, including dedicated ones, are already patched.

[short pause] [serious] There's fresh evidence behind yesterday's switch and WordPress urgency, too: a Chinese-speaking actor has exploited Zyxel GS1900 and WordPress flaws to steal sensitive data from nine hundred and ninety-six devices and more than eighteen thousand five hundred database records. And WordPress now has a separate core fix: 7 point 1 point 2, with backports to 4 point 7. An unauthenticated path traversal can make a site load a P-H-P file outside its theme folders; on some servers that becomes attacker code execution. [calm] Yesterday's update isn't the end of the job. Apply this new security release for your branch.

[curious] Application-generated images are another unexpected route to the server. Next.js has an ImageResponse flaw involving crafted S-V-G input: the feature generating social-preview images can become a server code-execution path. Check Vercel's advisory for the appropriate fix; no safe release number has been given here. Meanwhile, Bifrost, the open-source A-I gateway routing requests to more than twenty model providers, has an unauthenticated command-execution flaw reachable with a single web request. If you operate it, treat the gateway's reachable interface as urgent while checking the vendor's remediation.

[serious] There's a similarly direct route into Lantronix console-management equipment. CVE-2026-80155 bypasses authentication at the web upload endpoint, allowing sensitive configuration reads and arbitrary file uploads that lead to code execution. Update S-L-C eight thousand to 9 point 7 point 0 point 5, and E-M-G eighty-five hundred or seventy-five hundred to 9 point 7 point 0 point 1. All firmware versions of S-L-B eight-eight-two and S-L-C-x zero-two or zero-three are listed as affected, with no fixed build identified. I'd restrict access to that management portal rather than leave it reachable while waiting.

[short pause] [calm] Adobe's patch work spans Connect and Experience Manager Forms, with nine critical defects allowing code execution or privilege escalation. Forms J-E-E has an authorisation flaw allowing code execution without user interaction in versions through 6 point 5 point 25 and the long-term-support edition through service pack two. Apply Adobe's matching updates. Campaign Classic, its marketing automation platform, also has five maximum-severity flaws through 7 point 4 point 4 build nine-four-zero-one: four code-injection defects and one authorisation defect, all allowing code execution as the current user without interaction. Get that deployment into Adobe's remediation queue too.

For software embedded deeper in production, R-T-I Connext Professional is messaging middleware used in distributed systems. Its core libraries have a remotely reachable stack overflow rated ten. [serious] The specific fixes given are 7 point 7 point 0 point 1 for the 7 point 4 branch onwards, and 7 point 3 point 1 point 6 for the 7 point 0 branch. Older affected lines extend back to 4 point 3 x; the older fixed-build descriptions aren't precise enough to use as upgrade targets. Ask R-T-I for the supported build for your application.

[short pause] [calm] On endpoints, Chrome 154 fixes a hundred and eight vulnerabilities, including critical memory-safety defects. Schedule that rollout. Windows needs a little more care: Microsoft says September's security updates can break Always On V-P-N on some Windows 11 systems. I'd test an actual remote connection in the deployment ring rather than make a blanket decision to withhold security fixes. And the Thunderbird advisory circulating today is explicitly from twenty twenty-four, for 115 point 9—not a newly announced September fix.

[serious] Two ways of undermining protection round out the queue. CERT Coordination Center warns that vendor-signed U-E-F-I Shell applications can bypass Secure Boot through commands such as Memory Modify. The signature doesn't make every exposed capability safe. Separately, NightmareEclipse's BigDiskBuster proof of concept fills available disk space to stop Microsoft Defender installing platform and signature updates. It was published on September nineteenth; there's no patch or Microsoft advisory. [calm] If you're investigating stalled Defender updates, check disk exhaustion as a possible cause, not just whether the service is running.

[confident] I'd give the owners of externally reachable security appliances a protected change window today, with someone checking for compromise alongside the patch work. Keep a close eye on vendor guidance for the claimed PeopleSoft exploit, but don't turn an extortionist's claim into a confirmed technical finding. [cheerful] Hope your Wednesday gives you a quiet window to get it done.
