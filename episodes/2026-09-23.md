*ShinyHunters claims it stole FBI employee and applicant data, while Microsoft disrupts the EvilTokens phishing service. Four newly listed exploited vulnerabilities demand urgent appliance work, and WordPress needs another core update.*

[cheerful] Good morning, it's 5:22 AM. Wednesday, the twenty-third of September, twenty twenty-six.

[short pause] [serious] ShinyHunters says it's breached the FBI, and the FBI is investigating. The gang claims a new Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day opened internal services, with more than two terabytes stolen and employee and applicant data exposed, including agents' home addresses. Those are the attackers' claims, not a confirmed inventory. They're also insisting it isn't financially motivated: they want the bureau to correct its account of how they operate. [calm] Whatever the motive, if those addresses are genuine, the danger reaches families, not just accounts. I'd keep the counterintelligence implications separate from the gang's publicity campaign. One deserves serious attention; the other doesn't deserve free credibility.

[serious] Elsewhere, extortion is showing up where healthcare users expect working services. Sorami Consulting says connections to Elsevier's Evolve and Sherpath, along with ClinicalPharmacology and G-S-D-D interfaces, are being redirected to LAPSUS-linked splash pages. That establishes a redirection problem, not the full extent of any data theft. And BigCommerce customer data was accessed through a compromised application key held by Ribon Apps. [confident] If you use that integration, I'd review its access and revoke or replace the affected key in coordination with the provider. The shop platform doesn't have to be breached for its customer data to leave.

[curious] The same uncomfortable reach shows up at CrowdSec: attackers stole a hundred and seventy private GitHub repositories using an OAuth token taken from a former employee's computer through the TanStack npm supply-chain attack. Former employee, still-useful token. That's the bit I'd check in my own offboarding process. [serious] Developers integrating Twilio also have a malicious npm package to watch for, tw-pkgprobe-7731. It poses as a bug-bounty probe while attempting to harvest sensitive data; if it ran in a build environment, investigate which credentials it could reach.

[calm] And the consequences outlast the intrusion. Sweden's privacy regulator has fined systems provider Miljödata a hundred and eighty-three thousand dollars over inadequate security behind the August twenty twenty-five breach affecting two point two million people. Canada's regulator has separately opened an investigation into IDScan's security practices and whether victim notifications met federal privacy law. That's an investigation, not a finding.

[short pause] [cheerful] There is some disruption going the other way: Microsoft says the EvilTokens device-code phishing service compromised twelve thousand Microsoft accounts over a few months, using an A-I chatbot in its scam operation. The coordinated takedown seized fifty websites and disabled more than a hundred and fifty domains. [serious] Useful progress, but it doesn't recover an already stolen session. And researchers have demonstrated a different login trap: someone who already has privileged access can register a rogue external M-F-A provider and steal passwords during legitimate sign-ins. I'd audit changes to those provider registrations; the important qualification is that this starts with privileged access, not a stranger simply visiting the login page.

[curious] Sometimes the trusted starting point is just a website. SANS Internet Storm Center's Macfinger ClickFix investigation found injected scripts on several legitimate sites, extending a campaign documented earlier this month by Ransom-ISAC. A familiar site can still hand you an instruction you shouldn't run. Today's ISC Stormcast is also out; the concrete development here is that wider set of compromised sites.

[serious] Once inside Windows, CLOSEDQUORUM adds a different kind of delegation. The malware can ask four model providers to choose from predefined post-compromise actions, including credential and cryptocurrency-wallet theft. It isn't inventing unlimited powers; it's selecting from a menu the malware already has. [curious] Access to those models is becoming a security story of its own: Dark Reading describes more than eighty thousand relay servers masking users in China accessing frontier models in the United States, with model cloning suspected rather than established.

[calm] That helps explain the rush to control automated agents. Okta has announced agent lifecycle management and an A-I kill switch, while a House Democrat has proposed a CISA test programme giving critical-infrastructure operators free access to frontier models after water-sector attacks. It's proposed legislation, not an available service. Wider debate over rogue A-I behaviour is pushing the same question: who can stop the thing? And today's Wazuh coverage tackles the less glamorous visibility gap around shadow I-T. [confident] My take: a stop button's only useful if you know which agents exist and what authority they've got.

[long pause] [serious] Now to systems where attackers already have a working route in. Four additions to CISA's exploited catalogue carry a federal deadline of Friday, September twenty-fifth. Two are Check Point flaws, and they reach different parts of the estate. CVE-2026-85102 abuses certificate trust during V-P-N negotiation to let an unauthenticated remote attacker execute code on a Quantum Security Gateway. That's the gateway itself, not merely a stolen tunnel session.

[serious] The other, CVE-2026-93616, combines directory traversal with file upload on Security Management Server: reach its web service, upload a script and execute it without logging in. Check Point says targeted attacks happened on July twenty-third, and emergency hotfixes are now available. [confident] I'd put this ahead of routine change work. It's the management plane for your firewalls. Apply the management hotfix and follow Check Point's remediation for the gateway flaw separately; fixing one doesn't close the other.

[short pause] [serious] The next exposed control point is F5 BIG-IP A-P-M, CVE-2026-94127, which F5 confirms is being exploited. The crucial distinction: you're affected when A-P-M is an OAuth authorisation server, with an access policy and OAuth profile on a virtual server. Client-only or resource-server-only deployments without those authorisation-server profiles aren't affected. Malicious traffic can trigger a heap overflow and unauthenticated code execution, including in Appliance mode.

[calm] For the 21 point 1 branch, F5's named engineering hotfix is 21 point 1 point 0 point 2 point 0 point 30 point 22, E-N-G. For 17 point 5, it's 17 point 5 point 1 point 9 point 0 point 160 point 12, E-N-G; for 17 point 1, it's 17 point 1 point 3 point 5 point 0 point 41 point 14, E-N-G. Match the exact engineering hotfix to your branch. I'd establish that OAuth role before spending the morning guessing from the product name.

[serious] And the fourth is Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator, the central controller for software-defined wide-area networks. CVE-2026-93952 lets a remote attacker reach privileged internal functionality and potentially compromise the controller and its managed data. Hosted deployments, including Dedicated, have already been patched. [calm] On-premises affected ranges are 5 point 2 point 0 through 5 point 2 point 3 point 15, and 6 point 1 point 0 through 6 point 1 point 3 point 7. Also affected: 6 point 4 point 0 through 6 point 4 point 2 point 7, and 7 point 0 point 0 through 7 point 0 point 0 point 2. Get the vendor's remediation for your branch; those endpoints are vulnerable versions, not upgrade targets.

[short pause] [serious] There's fresh evidence behind yesterday's switch and WordPress urgency: a Chinese-speaking actor has exploited Zyxel GS1900 and WordPress vulnerabilities to steal sensitive data from nine hundred and ninety-six devices and more than eighteen thousand five hundred backend database records. That doesn't establish that today's new WordPress flaw was the route in. [calm] Separately, WordPress released 7 point 1 point 2 yesterday, with security backports to every branch as far back as 4 point 7. This is another core fix: unauthenticated path traversal can make a site load a P-H-P file outside its theme directories, and on some servers that becomes attacker-controlled code execution. If you did yesterday's update, there's another one to schedule. Annoying, yes. Still necessary.

[curious] While you're in WordPress, two plugin warnings need care rather than score-chasing. Give Tributes through 2 point 3 point 1 deserialises untrusted input, allowing unauthenticated P-H-P object injection. But there's no known usable object chain in that plugin: impact depends on another installed plugin or theme supplying one. [serious] The Meta Box administrator-escalation advisory has conflicting product details: its description names Meta Box A-I-O through 3 point 11 point 0, while the affected-product field names Frontend Submission through 4 point 5 point 6. Check both against the vendor's guidance rather than guessing which label is right.

[curious] A particularly nasty trust failure sits underneath applications using Erlang/O-T-P's S-S-L library. A peer answering a T-L-S 1 point 3 client can include a pre-shared-key extension the client never offered, and the client completes the handshake without validating the server certificate. It can return a successful socket even though the peer has no certificate or prior session. [serious] That's server impersonation dressed up as a successful secure connection. If your application uses that library, check CVE-2026-89422 against your runtime; fixed versions haven't been specified here.

[serious] For more direct code execution, Adobe Campaign Classic, the marketing-campaign platform, has four distinct critical code-injection flaws affecting versions through 7 point 4 point 4 build 9401. They allow unauthenticated remote execution in the current user's context without user interaction; all four score ten. Get Adobe's remediation before treating that build as safe. And Bifrost, an open-source gateway routing requests to more than twenty model providers, has an unauthenticated command-execution flaw reachable with a single H-T-T-P request. No fixed version is identified in that disclosure, so I'd restrict access while obtaining the vendor's fix.

[calm] Another service worth checking is Kompini Tankuam Places, in releases before November twenty-fifth, twenty twenty-five. Its password-recovery endpoint accepts a user identifier but doesn't bind the recovery token to that user. Change the identifier and an unauthenticated attacker can reset another account's password, including an administrator's. Check the deployed release against CVE-2026-93556 and obtain the corrected version.

[short pause] [serious] Below the application layer, CERT-C-C warns that vendor-signed U-E-F-I Shell applications can bypass Secure Boot through commands such as memory modify. Review whether those shells remain trusted on systems where that boot boundary matters. And NightmareEclipse's BigDiskBuster proof of concept, published September nineteenth, fills available disk space to stop Microsoft Defender installing platform and signature updates. There's no patch or CVE, and no Microsoft advisory. [confident] I'd check update freshness alongside free space rather than assume a running Defender service means current protection.

[calm] One date trap before you go: the Mozilla advisories circulating today are from twenty twenty-three and twenty twenty-four, not fresh September fixes. They cover Firefox 121 and 122, then 124, 124 point 0 point 1 and 126; Firefox for i-O-S 120 and 123, plus Focus for i-O-S 122 and 123. The Thunderbird notices span 115 point 5 through 115 point 11, including 115 point 8 point 1. Firefox E-S-R notices cover 115 point 6 through 115 point 9, then 115 point 9 point 1 and 115 point 11. There's also the older timing side-channel advisory for P-K-C-S number one, version one point five decryption depadding. Don't mistake those historical fixed releases for today's upgrade destinations.

[confident] I'd reserve emergency change capacity for the exploited control-plane flaws, and use configuration checks to decide which F5 systems actually need it. Keep room for that second WordPress core update rather than closing the ticket on yesterday's version.
