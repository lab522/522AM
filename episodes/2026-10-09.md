# FBI seizes Flax Typhoon tools, Cisco patches Nexus takeover

*Seven governments warn about Flax Typhoon as the FBI seizes domains behind its hacking tools. Cisco patches remote takeover flaws in Nexus switches, Atlassian exploitation widens, and a compromised Tensorlake package carries a dangerous credential-revocation trap.*

Good morning, it's 5:22 AM. Friday, the ninth of October, twenty twenty-six.

The scanner used against a South Carolina power company has lost its domains to the FBI. Seven domains were seized, supporting MicroScan and FishHub, tools operated by China-based Integrity Technology Group for Flax Typhoon. Seven governments, including the UK, issued a joint warning alongside the seizure. Court documents describe successful intrusions into organisations MicroScan had scanned. Not just reconnaissance, then.

MicroScan is a Python web application firing more than thirteen hundred testing scripts through a Mirai-based botnet. The actors also spray passwords at Exchange; once inside, they disguise SoftEther VPN as conhost dot exe or dllhost dot exe, and use DCSync to steal credentials. FishHub handles spear-phishing and data theft, with a portal that lets third parties read stolen mail without entering the victims' accounts. That's an unpleasantly convenient service.

I'd treat the seizure as disruption, not eviction. Patch the advisory's eight exploited flaws: five have just entered CISA's KEV catalogue, with US federal agencies due to fix them by Sunday. Listed fixes include ProFTPD 1 point 3 point 5a and ONLYOFFICE Document Server 5 point 6 point 3. For Strapi, it's 4 point 8 point 0; Struts branch fixes are 2 point 3 point 20 point 3, 2 point 3 point 24 point 3 or 2 point 3 point 28 point 1. BIND fixes are 9 point 9 point 7 dash P2 or 9 point 10 point 2 dash P3. Check for unexpected Active Directory replication too: patching doesn't recover stolen credentials.

From old flaws still earning their keep to new ones with a useful escape hatch: Cisco has fixed five critical NX-OS flaws that can give an unauthenticated remote attacker root on Nexus data centre switches, or force a reload. Crafted requests or packets reach validation failures, but only with the relevant optional feature active. NX-API is one route; the others involve NGOAM or MPLS OAM, with additional configuration requirements for some bugs.

Nexus 3000 and 9000 switches in standalone mode are the concern; Nexus 7000 and 9000 in ACI mode aren't affected by these five. Cisco knows of no exploitation. I'd check enabled features before booking a reboot: disabling an unused affected feature removes that route in. Use Cisco's Software Checker for the fixed release, or its temporary Live Protect shields while arranging the upgrade.

The wider batch also covers APIC and Meraki, plus a publicly disclosed Finesse SSRF. Cisco License On-Prem needs version 10 dash 202609, with no workaround; older Smart Software Manager-branded releases won't get patches, so those need migration.

That's the batch without known exploitation; yesterday's Atlassian story has moved the other way, with Previdian now counting a hundred and ninety attempts from thirty-two addresses across ten countries. VulnCheck has added the flaw to its exploited list after activity targeting Bamboo Data Center, though CISA still hasn't listed it. Meanwhile, at Pwn2Own Ireland, day two brought forty-five unique zero-days, and Ikotas Labs took three hundred thousand dollars for a Pixel 10 exploit chain on day three. Those targets were fully patched; there aren't fixes to install yet, and vendors have ninety days before disclosure.

A rather less welcome delivery arrived through npm: tensorlake, the TypeScript SDK for Tensorlake's cloud services, carried the Shai-Hulud credential-stealing worm in version 0 point 5 point 144. If it was installed, disable the malicious token monitor before revoking GitHub credentials—revocation can trigger deletion of the user's home directory—then rebuild from a trusted source, move to 0 point 5 point 145 and rotate exposed secrets. And not every alarming arrival is new: Mozilla's advisory backfill now reaches twenty nineteen and twenty twenty, with roughly sixty more old pages. No new patch job if your browsers and mail clients are current.

Before the weekend, I'd make sure any Tensorlake cleanup instructions include that revocation trap. And give Sunday's KEV deadline a real change window, not just a date on a ticket. Hope Friday leaves you a quiet evening.
