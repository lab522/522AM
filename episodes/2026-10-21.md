# PHP patches race the leak; certificate trust needs rebuilding

2026-10-21 · [listen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lab522/522AM/main/episodes/2026-10-21.mp3)

Brought to you by [Lab522](https://www.lab522.co.uk)

*Emergency PHP fixes are due today after details of two critical flaws leaked. Weak eMudhra certificate keys demand updates beyond browsers, while public exploits and cloud billing theft add to the response queue.*

Good morning, it's 5:22 AM. Wednesday, the twenty-first of October, twenty twenty-six.

Fourteen hundred UTC. That's when PHP plans to release emergency fixes for two critical remote code execution flaws, after embargoed details reportedly leaked. A video of a tool called PHP2Shell shows code running on webservers; watchTowr confirms the bugs are real and believes it's reproduced one. PHP rates both nine point two. CERT-EU says unauthenticated code execution may be possible.

The confirmed affected versions and fixed releases aren't announced yet, but CERT-EU warns the exposure could stretch back to PHP 5. That's potentially a lot of websites, including applications whose owners haven't thought about the underlying PHP installation in years. Sansec expects mass attacks within fifteen minutes of release. That's a prediction, not observed exploitation, but I'd have somebody ready at the keyboard rather than waiting for the next patch meeting.

Find the PHP owner and inventory those applications now. Back up sites and databases, then book the maintenance window. Watch PHP dot net: the maintainers may release early. If you can't patch an internet-facing application promptly, CERT-EU recommends isolating it from public networks until you can.

And while PHP needs new code, some software needs new trust. Certificate authority eMudhra has revoked eight intermediates whose private keys could be recovered by trying just two hundred and fifty-six numbers. One key was four. Not four followed by anything. Four. eMudhra blames a script error and similar labels on the weak and strong keys.

An attacker positioned to intercept traffic could forge certificates for legitimate sites. Browser vendors have pushed blocks, but clients that don't check revocation can remain exposed; I'd worry about those more than the browser. Rebuild Go programs with 1 point 27 point 3 or 1 point 26 point 10; older unsupported branches get no fix. Update static trust bundles, including Python certifi to 2026 point 10 point 19, and NSS to 3 point 131 point 1. Use the advisory's test endpoints to check what's still trusting those keys, including containers.

Back to fixes you can install already: Horizon3's new research concerns Oracle E-Business Suite, rather than its earlier Ivanti work, with unauthenticated SQL injection chaining into operating-system commands as the application account. No exploitation's reported; apply October's Critical Patch Update to affected Application Object Library web tiers on 12 point 2 point 3 through 12 point 2 point 15. And Horizon3 now has public exploit code for FortiSIEM, Fortinet's security-event platform: a gRPC path traversal becomes unauthenticated code execution. Upgrade 7 point 5 point 0 to 7 point 5 point 1 or later; meanwhile block TCP port twenty-seven thousand nine hundred and eighteen at the edge.

Those exposed services are exactly where ransomware crews keep finding work: a new joint advisory puts SafePay at more than six hundred victims since August twenty twenty-four. Check advisory AA26-293A's five exploited flaws against your edge-device patch queue, and hunt its indicators for hijacked admin accounts and remote-management activity. Automation's joining that workload too: Reuters says criminals using autonomous Cairn agents attempted more than twelve hundred websites and reached sites belonging to over a hundred companies. Check for exposed Cairn panels on your own and your testers' infrastructure, often on port eight thousand, and rotate secrets that passed through them.

But sometimes attackers want your AI budget rather than AI doing the attacking: Wiz found phished Google Cloud credentials used to attach attacker-owned projects to victims' billing accounts for mass Gemini usage. Bills reached tens to hundreds of thousands of dollars within forty-eight hours; check external project links and billing-admin changes, because the prompts weren't in victims' logs. From stolen cloud authority to trusted endpoint software, Lazarus's Operation FlashHole abused South Korea's mandatory CrossWeb EX banking software, compromising at least three organisations. In affected estates, hunt for fsquirt.exe launched beneath INISAFECrossWebEXSvc.exe and use Securelist's indicators; the chain leaves little on disk.

And on the update-channel compromise we covered on Saturday, AOMEI now confirms tampering but still hasn't identified or notified infected customers. Don't wait for an email: run its new detection tool and hunt hosts running AOMEI across April to July. The official response queue has moved too: yesterday's four Plex bugs are now in KEV, alongside SiteGuard and Siemens Software Center. Federal deadlines are the twenty-third of October for two Plex flaws and SiteGuard; the other entries are due the third of November, with compromise checks expected under the directive.

That exploited queue matters more than sheer volume: Talos counts fourteen hundred and fifteen Microsoft advisories this month, attributing the surge to AI mining old code, while only three September flaws had detected in-the-wild exploitation. Prioritise what's exploitable in your environment, not whichever dashboard has the biggest number. For operational technology, CISA also has immediate actions for Hitachi Energy's MicroSCADA X SYS600 electricity-control software, covering CSV formula handling and authentication flaws. Follow the version-specific advisory and keep exported CSVs untrusted. And one welcome subtraction from the queue: Mozilla's overnight flood is a republication of its twenty fourteen and twenty fifteen advisory archive. Suppress those old feed entries, not today's genuine browser and NSS work.

I'd make sure today's PHP window has a named decision-maker who can authorise isolation if patching stalls. And give the certificate checks an owner outside the browser team; that's where this could linger. Have a good Wednesday.
